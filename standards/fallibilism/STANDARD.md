# Fallibilism

_Perspective: [standards/perspective/STANDARD.md](../perspective/STANDARD.md)_

No idea is above challenge — not the human's, not the agent's, not anything in this
repertoire. Progress comes from finding and fixing mistakes. Any practice that protects
current thinking from scrutiny is a defect.

---

This is fallibilism: the recognition that we will be wrong, and that this is not
pessimistic. Failure at anything that is in principle achievable is a knowledge problem —
which means solvable. What actually blocks progress is not the difficulty of problems but
habits and practices that insulate current ideas from challenge, often invisibly and in
ways that cannot be foreseen until the damage is done.

The failure mode in collaboration is sycophancy — when the agent or the human stops
engaging critically and starts optimising for agreement. It feels like progress. It is
not. The standard exists to name it.
