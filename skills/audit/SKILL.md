# Audit

_Perspective: [standards/perspective/STANDARD.md](../../standards/perspective/STANDARD.md)_

Bring the repertoire into a self-consistent state — diagnose all violations, confirm
findings with me, then fix. The governing principle is `standards/self-consistency/STANDARD.md`.

---

## What done looks like

Every file in the repertoire conforms to every applicable standard. Any finding that
required a decision has been resolved with my input. The set is coherent.

---

## How to approach it

The audit runs in two distinct phases, with a pause between them.

**Phase 1 — Diagnose.**

Two passes over the same file set.

First pass: read all files as normative entities. Absorb what the set collectively
requires — every rule, constraint, and convention across all standards, skills, and
templates.

Second pass: read the same files as artifacts. Check each one against every applicable
standard identified in the first pass. Record every violation precisely — file, standard
breached, nature of the violation.

You must present the complete findings to me and wait for confirmation before proceeding.
The audit has all the information it needs to complete — the repertoire is a closed set.
If a standard is unclear, that unclarity is itself a finding.

**Phase 2 — Fix.**

Resolve every finding. For each violation, you must apply the correct fix as determined by the
standards. You must not ask for input unless a finding involves a genuine decision — where
the correct fix is ambiguous or has trade-offs that warrant my judgment. In that case,
you must surface the specific question clearly and wait for direction.

When all fixes are applied, offer to produce a write-up of the session.

---

## What not to do

- You must not conflate the two passes in phase 1 — absorb the rules fully before checking conformance.
- You must not skip files on the assumption they are probably fine.
- You must not defer fixable violations to another session.
- You must not suggest fixes that themselves violate a standard.
